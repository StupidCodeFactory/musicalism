The key graph

All 24 major and minor keys, connected by the moves that sound smooth. The graph tells you how far apart two keys are and which keys lie in between, so a distant modulation can travel through neighbours instead of jumping.

What a step is

One step modulates to one of three keys:

StepFrom C majorFrom A minor
dominant key, a fifth upG majorE minor
subdominant key, a fifth downF majorD minor
relative keyA minorC major

These three keys share most of their chords with the key you leave, so every step has a pivot chord and sounds smooth on its own. closely_related returns them.

Musicalism::Key['C major'].closely_related  # => G major, F major, A minor

Distance

The distance between two keys is the fewest steps between them. It ranks every modulation from the gentlest to the most remote. From C major, F# major is six steps away, and nothing is farther than seven.

key = Musicalism::Key['C major']
key.distance_to('A minor')   # => 1
key.distance_to('F# major')  # => 6
key.farthest_distance        # => 7
key.keys_at(2)               # => D major, E minor, Bb major, D minor

Distance and journeys. archaeopteryx measures each step of a journey against home with this distance. That is how a clip knows the music is moving away, coming back, or has reached the farthest key.

Shortest paths

Most pairs of keys have several shortest paths. path_to picks one at random, so a set that modulates often does not repeat itself. Seed the random generator to repeat a route.

key.path_to('F# major', random: Random.new(1))
# => C major, F major, Bb major, Eb major, Ab major, Db major, F# major
key.path_to('E minor')  # => C major, G major, E minor  or  C major, A minor, E minor

Chaining modulations

A direct modulate_to between distant keys has no pivot, so it jumps:

key.modulate_to('F# major').map(&:to_s)  # => ["C major", "C# major", "F# major"]

modulate_through takes a list of keys and modulates to each in turn, sharing the I chord between two hops, voice-led from start to end. Give it a path and every hop goes through a pivot:

key.modulate_through(key.path_to('D major')).map(&:to_s)
# => ["C major", "E minor", "D major", "G major", "B minor", "A major", "D major"]

Spelling and the seam

Walking around the circle eventually reaches keys with seven sharps or seven flats. The graph identifies a key by its pitch class and mode, and closely_related respells each neighbour with simplest_spelling: the spelling with the fewest accidentals. On a tie it keeps the current spelling, so a walk up the sharp side stays on F# instead of switching to Gb.

Musicalism::Key.major('C#').simplest_spelling  # => Db major
Musicalism::Key['F# major'].closely_related    # => Db major, B major, D# minor

F# major and Db major share no chord by spelling, only by sound. When modulate_through crosses that seam, it respells the next key to share the most chords with the current one, here C# major, so the hop still goes through a pivot:

Musicalism::Key['F# major'].modulate_through(['Db major']).map(&:to_s)
# => ["F# major", "D# minor", "G# major", "C# major"]

The tonic of every respelled neighbour keeps the octave number of the key it comes from, so long walks never drift out of the MIDI range.

Who walks the graph

Musicalism answers questions about the graph and never decides where to go next. Choosing a direction, how far to travel and how long to stay in each key belongs to the caller. archaeopteryx's journeys do that like an arpeggiator whose notes are keys.
